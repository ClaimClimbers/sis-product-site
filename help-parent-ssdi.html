How to help a parent apply for SSDI

Yes. You can help your parent apply for SSDI. You can gather information. You can sit through the online application or a phone appointment. You can organize their medical file. Your parent generally must sign the application and the medical release, not you. Form SSA-1696 is only needed if they appoint you as an official representative. Do not delay filing solely to chase every record.

Adult children and spouses who search "help parent apply for SSDI" usually want role clarity first. What can you do? What still belongs to your parent? How do you stay on the right side of consent and privacy?

What can a family helper do, and what must the parent still do?

You can prepare lists. You can keep calendars. You can sit through the application with your parent. You can organize paper. Your parent still owns account access, signatures, and the facts of their own claim.

In practice that means you can pull together provider names and phone numbers. You can print the Adult Disability Starter Kit checklist. You can book the phone appointment. You can take notes while they answer. You can help them create a my Social Security account. Per Social Security's own helper guidance, you cannot create that account for them.

Your parent (the claimant) generally must:

  • Sign in to their own my Social Security account when the path requires it
  • Sign the disability application
  • Sign Form SSA-827, the medical release that lets Social Security request records

You are not their lawyer by sitting at the kitchen table. You are not filing "as" them unless they have given you a form of authority Social Security recognizes for that step. Keep the work practical and present: calendars, forms prep, status checks with them in the room.

If you are carrying someone else's paper and want a short product path aimed at helpers, start at for family. That page is the audience door. This article stays on how to help.

Informal help vs appointing a representative (Form SSA-1696)

Informal family help does not require Form SSA-1696. Use SSA-1696 only if your parent wants you officially appointed to do business with Social Security on the claim.

Social Security's third-party apply page is direct: friends and relatives may help someone apply, and you do not need to be appointed as a representative just to help. Appointment is a separate choice when the claimant wants an authorized representative on the file.

Informal family helper Appointed representative (SSA-1696)
Purpose Help gather information, complete forms with the claimant, attend with them Officially act and do business with Social Security on the claim
Needed just to help apply? No Only if they want an official appointment
Who signs the application The applicant, in ordinary cases Still claim-specific; do not assume you can sign for them
Access to claim information Limited without appointment Broader representative access after appointment processes
Fees Unpaid family help has no SSA fee rules to navigate Representatives who charge fees follow Social Security's fee rules

A power of attorney for banking or health care is not the same thing as an SSA representative appointment. If your parent wants you to act officially with Social Security, use the appointment process Social Security publishes for Form SSA-1696. Edge cases (incapacity, a mark instead of a signature, other legal capacity questions) are not DIY legal advice. Read Social Security's current instructions, or talk with a qualified representative or attorney if the situation is not ordinary.

This comparison is about roles, not about whether a lawyer helps or hurts a claim. Choose representation when you want an advocate. Choose informal help when you want a family member at the table.

What information should you help your parent gather before applying?

Help your parent assemble the information Social Security already asks for on its adult disability starter materials: treating sources, medications, work history, education, and notes about daily function they can confirm themselves.

A practical starter list:

  • Doctors, clinics, hospitals, therapists, and imaging centers, with addresses and approximate dates
  • Current medications and who prescribed them
  • Jobs from roughly the past 15 years (employer names, dates, duties), plus education
  • Medical records already in hand: visit notes, imaging reports, therapy notes, medication lists
  • Short notes your parent can confirm about sitting, standing, lifting, concentration, and bad days

How do you help with the SSDI application itself?

Help with the application by sitting with your parent, choosing the correct "who is completing this" path, and making sure they sign what Social Security requires.

Social Security's five-step helper post walks through the online path in plain terms. Before you begin, the application asks who is completing it:

  • If you are with your parent and they can sign, select that they are applying for themselves. They can electronically sign when they have a my Social Security account.
  • If they are not with you, or cannot create or sign in to their account, select that you are helping someone who is not with you. Social Security then mails the application and Form SSA-827 for their signature. Without those signed forms, the agency cannot process the application and make a medical decision.

If online is not workable, call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213 (Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time; TTY 1-800-325-0778) or use a local office appointment. Print the Adult Disability Starter Kit checklist and worksheet so the phone call or visit goes faster. That kit is prep, not legal advice on how to answer every field.

After filing, help track status with your parent present. The medical release (Form SSA-827) still needs their signature so Social Security can ask the clinics you listed.

If you later see an Upload Documents button, that is for sending copies your parent already holds. It is not the same as SSA-827. Uploading does not mean every clinic has answered. Security in Social does not upload to Social Security for your family. For the three-path plain words (own copies vs SSA-827 vs Upload), see the gather medical records for SSDI guide.

How should a family helper handle medical records and privacy?

Treat the records as your parent's, not yours. Get appropriate authorization before you request copies, keep an organized personal set offline from public demos, and never paste live private health information into a public form.

Under HIPAA, patients (and people who qualify as personal representatives) can request copies from treating sources. The exact paperwork varies by clinic. Keep your role honest: you are helping them request and organize, not claiming ownership of the file.

  • Store copies in a place only the family helper team can reach
  • Use SAMPLE language only on public demo surfaces
  • Do not upload live charts into a public marketing form
  • Production handling for paid reviews follows the site privacy policy and private review path

How do you talk about daily limits without inventing the story?

Help your parent describe real limits in their own words. Do not coach them to exaggerate, and do not invent functional language the clinics never wrote down.

Ask concrete questions they can answer: How long can you sit before you need to stand? What happens on a bad day at the grocery store? How often do you miss appointments because of symptoms? Write what they say. If a form later asks you for a third-party function report (Form SSA-3380 is one Social Security may send to people who know the claimant), answer honestly and specifically from what you have seen. Do not fill holes with hopeful wording.

When the stack of copies is in hand and the question shifts from "how do we help apply" to "what does this file still lack," that is a different job. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for SSDI for my parent?

You can help them complete the application, but they generally must sign. If they cannot be present online, Social Security's "helping someone who is not with me" path mails the application and medical release for their signature. That is not a substitute for legal authority when a signature edge case requires it. Follow Social Security's published instructions.

Do I need to be an appointed representative to help?

No. Informal help does not require Form SSA-1696. Use that form only if your parent wants you officially appointed to do business with Social Security on the claim. Helping at the kitchen table and acting as an appointed representative are different roles.

What medical records should a family helper gather?

Dated treating-source records your parent already has or can authorize you to request: visit notes, imaging reports, therapy notes, and medication lists. Use the gather guide for request mechanics through portals and desks. Do not delay filing solely to chase every page. List known sources and keep collecting after you apply.

How do we protect my parent's privacy while I help?

Keep their records off public demos. Use proper authorization for copies. On Security in Social's public pages, SAMPLE language only. Production handling for a paid review follows the privacy policy and private review path. Never invent private details for an example.
