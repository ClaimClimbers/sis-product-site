For you · the person applying

You are applying for SSDI.

You need a doctor’s letter about what you can and cannot do at work. Records specialists build the packet from your medical file. A licensed doctor (MD or DO) reviews and signs, or says no when the records are not enough. We do not promise Social Security will approve your claim.

Plain language

What these letters mean.

Doctor’s letter about what you can still do (MSS)

A doctor-signed written opinion about work limits. The hearing file can use it. It is not a promise you will be approved.

Physical work-limits letter (physical RFC) · $450

Sit, stand, walk, lift, carry, and breaks, only when dated records support numbers. If a part of the file has no support, that part stays open or we decline.

Mental work-limits letter (mental MRFC) · $300

How mental limits affect work for an SSDI hearing. Same rule: language tied to the file, then a licensed doctor signs.

Records screen · $149

A records review first. If the file cannot carry an opinion, we stop. A paid screen that refuses is the product working, not a failed sale.

The clinical gate

  • A licensed MD or DO is the only person who signs.
  • Specialists build the packet. They do not sign.
  • If the record cannot support the opinion, we decline.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee of SSDI approval.
  • Not a percent of your benefits.
  • Not a consultative exam or independent medical exam.
  • Not a product for a different benefits system. This is SSDI.
