How we build a medical record review for Social Security

We help seniors and families understand a medical file before they apply. Success means you know what the file shows, what is still missing, and a clear next step. One front door: Start a record review.

Who does the work

Specialists assemble the file and explain it.

People on our team put the dated papers in order. They say what the file shows and what is still missing, in words you can follow at the kitchen table.

A doctor does not sign this medical record review. We do not sell doctor letters. We do not ask a physician to attest to this review.

File strength

Three words. Strong, Mixed, or Thin.

When we name how complete the stack is, we use only those words. We do not put a percent on the file. We do not show a Social Security scoreboard of approval odds.

Strong

The dated papers are enough to see the treatment and the limits already written down.

Mixed

Part of the story is in the file. Some providers, dates, or limits are still missing.

Thin

The stack is too small, too old, or too thin to answer the questions. We say so.

Left out on purpose

Letters are not for sale here.

Not in this review

A letter for a doctor to sign. A promise Social Security will approve the claim. A percent of your benefits as a fee. A diagnosis we invent because the papers are thin.

How the words are written

Specialists write the explanation. We do not market this as a bot that decides your claim. You get a calm read from people, in everyday words.
